About

A small firm, and the person accountable for it.

Trouvus is a Toronto company building deterministic AI systems for decisions that carry consequences. This page says who is behind it, and discloses the relationship between this company and the foundation whose framework it cites.

The principal

Dr. Mitt Nowshade Kabir

Co-founder and CEO of Trouvus, and Founding Director of the Canadian Center for AI Entrepreneurship.

He has spent three decades on the same question from three sides: how organisations actually absorb new technology, rather than how they say they will.

As a researcher, his work sits in knowledge management, innovation systems and industrial digital transformation — including work with Elias G. Carayannis on tacit knowledge and organisational competitiveness, and the book Knowledge-Based Social Entrepreneurship (Palgrave Macmillan, 2019). His recent chapter Cognitive Industry 4.0 Reimagined — Human-Centered Intelligence and Organizational Resilience in Post-Industrial Systems proposes co-adaptive intelligence, real-time mutual learning between people and machines, as the mechanism by which organisational resilience emerges. It is the scholarly foundation of the governance work CCAIE now publishes. He has served as an adjunct professor of digital transformation.

As an operator, he designs and delivers applied AI programmes for enterprises and institutions. In August 2026 he designed and led a ten-day executive programme for thirty leaders of an industrial holding, which produced five pilots at investment-decision quality — and a published finding about why organisations stall after training that has since reshaped how both organisations sell.1

As an entrepreneur, he has founded and scaled several technology ventures. He was chairman of Rusbiz, a global B2B marketplace that grew to a reported 300,000 users, and founder of Flerika, an AI-enabled social commerce platform — working on recommendation engines, semantic search and data-driven personalisation well before those became standard.

He chairs and speaks at international conferences on artificial intelligence and robotics, has advised governments on digital innovation and e-government policy, and mentors and invests in early-stage AI ventures.

The thesis

Where this company's argument comes from.

The 2026 TAKE keynote in Lisbon argues that as intelligence becomes abundant the scarce resource becomes verification — and that the organisations which win the next decade will be those that can prove what their systems produce, not those with the most capable models.2

Everything Trouvus builds is an attempt to make that argument operational. If the argument is wrong, the company is wrong, and we would rather that be legible than buried in a mission statement.

Disclosure

Trouvus and the CCAIE Foundation.

This site cites the Octopus Protocol as a public, citable, vendor-neutral standard, and treats two of its principles as constraints we can be held to. You should know the relationship before you weigh that.

The two organisations are separate, and the same person leads both. The Canadian Center for AI Entrepreneurship is a foundation that publishes governance research and delivers executive programmes. Trouvus is a company that builds systems. Dr. Kabir is Founding Director of the first and co-founder and CEO of the second.

So the framework we cite is authored by our own principal. It is genuinely vendor-neutral in content — it names no supplier, requires no technology, and is satisfiable by systems that are not ours — but it is not third-party validation, and we do not present it as such. It is a published position we can be measured against, which is a weaker claim than independence and a stronger one than a sales sheet.

The August 2026 executive programme was delivered through CCAIE, not Trouvus. It appears in our evidence because the principal designed and led it, and that is stated wherever the figures appear.

The firm

What we are not.

We are small. We publish no team size, no founding-date milestone, no client logos and no testimonials — not as a stylistic choice, but because we have no verified ones to show and would have to invent them.

There is one engagement at scale on the evidence page, published with the limits of what it established. One system with revenue. One available for a funded pilot. That is the whole inventory, and the site is built so you can check it.
