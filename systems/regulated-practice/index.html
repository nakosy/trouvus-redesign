Status, stated plainly

This is architecture and an offer, not a track record.

No client deployment of this system exists. We are describing what we have designed and what we will build under a funded pilot, and we would rather say so than imply a case study we do not have.

Offering the pilot openly is the stronger position anyway. A firm that shows you an anonymised success story is asking you to trust a claim you cannot check. A firm that tells you it has not shipped this yet is telling you something verifiable — and everything below is a commitment you can hold it to.

The custody problem

The speed is not the hard part.

Retrieval and drafting over a professional's own corpus is not technically difficult any more. What stops regulated practices adopting it is that the useful version requires privileged material to leave their custody — to a vendor, a model provider, or a jurisdiction whose rules they cannot answer for.

So the design constraint is not capability. It is that nothing leaves.

PropertyCommitment
DeploymentThe client's own hardware, or a single-tenant instance in a jurisdiction the client chooses.
EgressNo client data reaches Trouvus or any model provider.
ProvenanceEvery generated assertion links to an authenticated source document.
ConfidenceEach output carries a confidence score, with the mandatory-review threshold set before deployment rather than negotiated during it.
Sign-offA named practitioner must sign off before use. Not advisory.
LanguageBuilt for bilingual, multi-script practice.
ExitClient data and indexes remain client property and are exportable on exit.
The exit clause is deliberate. A knowledge system that cannot be taken away from its vendor is not an asset, it is a dependency. Ask every supplier in this category what happens to your indexes when you leave, and compare the answers.
Before a pilot

Five questions your regulator will ask.

The Octopus Protocol is general. Applying it inside a regulated profession requires an annex answering five questions in that profession's own terms.1 We answer them with you before anything is built, because the answers change the system rather than describe it.

  • 01What counts as an authenticated source in this practice?
  • 02Who may hold accountability for an agent's output — and does professional regulation restrict that to particular licence-holders?
  • 03What must the audit record contain to satisfy this profession's regulator?
  • 04Where may data reside, and under whose custody, given the confidentiality obligations that apply?
  • 05At what confidence threshold does human review become mandatory rather than advisory?

A profession that has answered these five has a standard. Any vendor able to satisfy the answers meets it — including one that is not us. That is what makes it a standard rather than a sales sheet.

What a funded pilot is

Bounded, measured, and yours at the end.

One practice area, one corpus, a defined stop condition and a named practitioner answerable for sign-off. It begins the way every engagement begins — by measuring how the work is done now.
